Audio 1: Thanksgiving in the United States

Thanksgiving in the United States

Thanksgiving is a special holiday in the United States. It is on the fourth Thursday of November every year. On this day, families and friends come together to eat a big meal and say thank you for the good things in their lives.

The holiday started a long time ago. In 1621, a group of people called the Pilgrims had a feast with the Wampanoag people in Massachusetts. The Pilgrims came from England by ship. Their first year was very hard, and the Wampanoag helped them learn to grow food. After the harvest, they shared a meal to celebrate together.

Today, the most famous food at Thanksgiving is turkey. Many families cook a big turkey in the oven for several hours. They also make mashed potatoes, stuffing, corn, and green beans. For dessert, people often eat pumpkin pie. Cranberry sauce is another popular dish, and it is red and sweet.

Many people also enjoy fun traditions. In the morning, some people watch a big parade on television. Others play football in the yard or watch a game with their family. Before dinner, many families say what they are grateful for. A child might say, "I am grateful for my family," and a grandmother might say, "I am grateful for my health."

Thanksgiving is also a time to help other people. Some families give food to people who need it. Others work as volunteers in their town. The day after Thanksgiving is called Black Friday, and many people go shopping because there are big sales in the stores.

In the end, Thanksgiving is not only about food. It is about being together, being kind, and remembering to say thank you every day. It is a warm and happy day for everyone.
